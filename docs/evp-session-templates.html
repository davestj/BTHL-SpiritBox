EVP Session Templates Copy-ready dialogue scripts for an active spirit-box session

These are starting scripts, not rules. Read them aloud at a calm, even pace. After every question, stop talking and wait 5–10 seconds — that silent window is when a response can form and be transcribed. Lines marked SAY are spoken; (pause) marks the listening window.

Pacing tip. The sweep dwell time sets how fast frequencies change. A dwell of ~30 ms with a 5–10 s wait after each question is a good default. Slow down if responses are fragmentary.

1 · Pre-session checklist

  • RTL-SDR connected; FM (or chosen) sweep running and audible.
  • Whisper model loaded (base.en or better).
  • EMF-390 connected; note the baseline reading.
  • Capture mode chosen (Interactive for a Q&A session).
  • Recording armed if you want a WAV + session archive.
  • Phones silenced; one designated speaker.

2 · Opening / introduction

Establish who you are and your intent. Calm and respectful gets better engagement than demands.

SAY: "Today is [date]. The time is [time]. We are at [location]. My name is [name], and I'm here with [names]."

SAY: "We're using a device that lets you speak through radio sounds. If anyone is here with us, you can use it to talk to us."

(pause 10 seconds)

SAY: "We mean you no harm. We'd just like to talk. Can you hear my voice?"

(pause)

3 · Rapport / permission

SAY: "Is it alright if we ask you some questions?" (pause)

SAY: "If you'd like us to stay, say stay. If you'd like us to leave, say leave." (pause)

SAY: "Can you say my name?" (pause)

4 · Core question sets

Keep each question short. Favor answers the sweep can render in one or two words.

Yes / No

SAY: "Are we welcome here?" (pause)

SAY: "Are you alone?" (pause)

SAY: "Do you want us to leave?" (pause)

Identity

SAY: "What is your name?" (pause)

SAY: "How old are you?" (pause)

SAY: "Are you a man or a woman?" (pause)

Intent & environment

SAY: "Why are you here?" (pause)

SAY: "Is this your home?" (pause)

SAY: "What year do you think it is?" (pause)

SAY: "Is there something you want us to know?" (pause longer, 15s)

Number / count (good for verifiable answers)

SAY: "How many of you are here?" (pause)

SAY: "Count to three for me." (pause)

5 · Following up on a response

When you hear or see a transcribed word, acknowledge and build on it — this is how a session becomes a conversation rather than scattered fragments.

SAY: "I heard you say [word]. Did you say [word]?" (pause)

SAY: "Thank you. Can you tell me more?" (pause)

6 · Provocation — use with caution

Avoid taunting. Provocation ("show yourself if you can!") is a popular TV trope but tends to produce noise, distress, and unreliable results. Respectful, curious questions yield clearer, more repeatable responses. Skip provocation entirely if anyone present is uneasy.

7 · Closing

SAY: "We're going to stop now. Thank you for talking with us." (pause)

SAY: "We're not taking anything with us, and we're not leaving anything behind. This conversation is over for tonight."

SAY: "The time is now [time]. Ending the session."

Then stop recording and File → Save Transcript… Review the log while it's fresh.

Tips for legible responses

Short questions, single ideas.
5–10 s of silence after every question.
One speaker — name the others, but let one person drive.
Repeat answers back to confirm and continue the thread.
Call out timestamps when something stands out.
Stay skeptical — favor relevant, repeatable, EMF-correlated responses.
